Comprehensive Technical Implementation Report

Project Name: The Last Corridor

Report Scope: Detailed System Architecture, Game Mechanics, and Narrative Implementation

Development Period: Late August 2026 – Late September 2026

This document serves as an exhaustive technical breakdown of the systems, features, and mechanics implemented in The Last Corridor. Over the past several weeks, the project has transitioned from basic environments to a fully playable, immersive, and terrifying psychological horror experience. Below is a detailed explanation of the core pillars of the game's architecture.

1. Advanced Artificial Intelligence (The Stalker/Joker)

The primary antagonist is driven by a highly complex, state-based NavMesh AI system designed to keep the player in a constant state of paranoia.

  • State Machine Architecture: The AI operates on multiple behavioral states: Patrol, Chase, InvestigateSound, Tease, Stunned, and CinematicEscape. This allows the monster to react dynamically rather than just mindlessly walking toward the player.
  • Field of View (FOV) & Line of Sight: The monster utilizes raycasting and dot-product mathematical checks to determine if the player is within its visual cone (e.g., 90-degree FOV). It ignores the player if the player is hidden behind walls or crouched behind specific cover objects.
  • Dynamic Audio Investigation: If the player sprints or triggers an environment noise (like dropping an item or walking on glass), the AI instantly switches to InvestigateSound, calculating the NavMesh path to the exact coordinate of the noise.
  • Tease & Ambush Mechanics: To build suspense, the AI occasionally teleports to a designated "tease" location just out of the player's direct sight, playing a sound or casting a shadow, and then disappearing when approached.

2. Persistent World State & Save System

A robust save system was built from the ground up using JSON serialization and Unity's PlayerPrefs to ensure the game remembers everything between sessions and deaths.

  • Transform Serialization: The SaveManager logs the exact X, Y, Z coordinates of the player. Upon loading, the system uses Physics.SyncTransforms() to ensure the player doesn't clip through the floor.
  • Inventory & World State Tracking: We implemented a collectedItemsCSV string system. When the player picks up a key or an item, its unique ID is saved. If the player dies and the scene reloads, the RespawnManager reads this list and destroys those items in the world so they cannot be duplicated.
  • Dropped Item Logic: If a player drops an item (like a flashlight) right before dying, the exact position of that dropped item is saved. On respawn, the game physically moves the item to that location so the player can retrieve it.

3. The "3 Lives" & Respawn Mechanics

Instead of a standard "Game Over" screen on every death, the game uses a cinematic 3-lives system to increase tension.

  • Drag to Basement Sequence: Upon the first and second deaths, control is stripped from the player. A customized cinematic plays where the camera falls to the floor, blinks heavily using UI alpha fades, and the player is dragged backward into the darkness by the Stalker.
  • Dynamic Spooky Text: The RespawnManager handles the screen fade. Based on livesRemaining, it shows different terrifying messages using rich text formatting (e.g., "HE IS GETTING CLOSER... YOU ARE RUNNING OUT OF TIME" with a dark crimson red font).
  • Safe Zone Teleportation: After the death cinematic, the scene is reloaded, and the player wakes up safely in the Basement using a mathematically interpolated camera "stand-up" animation, giving them a chance to try again.

4. Cinematic Control Managers

To give the game a "Movie-like" feel, several dedicated Cinematic Managers were programmed to handle complex camera and UI manipulations without breaking player movement logic.

  • IntroCinematicManager & VideoPlaylist: The game starts with a terrifying projector sequence. We engineered a bulletproof static boolean logic (replacing older PlayerPrefs logic) to guarantee the 3-minute intro plays exactly once per session. The player's camera is locked with a sluggish, restricted rotation to simulate waking up dizzy, while heavy breathing audio plays.
  • EscapeCinematicManager: The climax of the game involves escaping through a door while the stalker chases you. We implemented a dynamic Vector3.MoveTowards logic that automatically pulls the camera backward along the Z-axis while applying Perlin Noise camera shake. This prevented earlier bugs where the camera would clip through walls or freeze the enemy incorrectly.

5. Psychological Horror Atmosphere & Puzzles

Immersion is the most critical aspect of the game, achieved through precise audio-visual synchronization.

  • Dynamic Headbob & Footsteps: The PlayerController uses a sine wave mathematical function (Mathf.Sin) tied to the character's velocity to bob the camera up and down. Footstep audio pitch changes dynamically based on whether the player is crouching, walking, or sprinting.
  • Grandfather Clock Puzzle: A highly complex script that manipulates time. When triggered, the hands of the clock spin wildly backwards using Transform.Rotate multiplied by Time.deltaTime. The room lights instantly turn blood red, and a creepy sound plays in reverse, directly alerting the Stalker to the player's location.
  • Exhaustion & Heavy Breathing: The PlayerStats script tracks stamina. If the player sprints too much or experiences a cinematic shock, a heavy breathing audio source is triggered, simulating panic attacks that drown out other environmental sounds.

Conclusion

The combination of these highly technical systems—state-machine AI, persistent JSON serialization, complex camera mathematics, and dynamic audio manipulation—transforms The Last Corridor from a simple walking simulator into a highly advanced, responsive, and terrifying psychological horror game. The architecture is modular, bug-tested, and ready for the final polishing phases.
